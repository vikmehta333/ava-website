# The 48-Hour Follow-Up Sequence That Keeps Leads From Going Cold

If you saw the post: you don't need an AI strategy, you need one automated follow-up. Most small businesses don't lose a lead because the product's wrong — they lose it because nobody followed up fast enough, long enough, or at all. This is that sequence, built out step by step so you can set it up yourself, or hand it to us and we'll set it up for you.

## Why 48 hours is the window that matters

A lead is warmest the moment they raise their hand — a form fill, a call, a DM. Every hour after that, the odds of a reply drop. By 48 hours, most businesses have either booked the lead or lost them to whoever answered first. The sequence below isn't about following up more; it's about following up on a fixed schedule so no lead depends on someone remembering to check an inbox.

## Write the sequence before you touch any software

Same rule as always: write out exactly what gets said and when, in plain sentences, before you pick a tool.

**The five touches:**
1. *Minute 5:* "Thanks for reaching out to [Business Name] — got your message. [Answer the obvious question, or:] What's the best number to reach you at?"
2. *Hour 1 (if no reply):* "Still there? I've got [two specific times] open this week if you'd like to grab one — no need to call back and forth."
3. *Hour 24 (if no reply, switch channel):* Text got no reply → send the same offer by email, or vice versa. "Wanted to follow up in case the text/email got buried — happy to find a time that works."
4. *Hour 48 (last call):* "Last note on this — if now's not the right time, no worries at all. Reply anytime and we'll pick it back up."
5. *Hour 48+ (resolution):* Either the lead is booked, or it's marked cold in whatever you use to track leads. Nothing sits open past hour 48 with no next step.

Write your own version of each message before moving on. A script that sounds like you is the difference between "automated" and "obviously automated."

## Pick the tool that matches your size

Same categories as always — pick based on what you already run, not what's trendiest:

- **Already have a CRM or booking system** — most (Housecall Pro, Jobber, a dental/medical PM system) support scheduled follow-up sequences out of the box. Turn that on before adding anything new.
- **No CRM yet** — a combined texting platform (the category Podium or Birdeye sit in) can run a timed sequence like this one without custom setup.
- **Comfortable with a bit of setup** — a form tool wired through Zapier or Make to a texting service (Twilio) gives full control over timing and channel-switching for close to no monthly cost.

If you're not sure which category fits, that's what a free audit is for.

## Set the handoff rule

Decide up front what the sequence is never allowed to answer on its own:

- Any question about price beyond your standard rate
- Any complaint or negative tone
- A reply that doesn't match one of the expected responses

One sentence, given to whoever covers the phone or inbox: *"If it's not a scheduling question, a person replies within the hour."* The sequence's job is to keep the lead warm between touches — not to close every conversation by itself.

## Test it on yourself first

Fill out your own form. Send yourself a test lead. Read all five messages back to back as if you were the person waiting on a reply. If any of them read stiff or robotic, rewrite before a real lead sees it.

## Measure the one number that matters

Not open rates — track what share of leads are **booked or marked cold by hour 48**, versus sitting open with no next step. That's the number the whole sequence exists to move. If it isn't moving, the timing or the wording needs work, not a longer sequence.
